# Relay demo guide

## A. The 30-second walkthrough

Start on **Discover** (open the app, or use **AR → Reset demo**).

1. **Discover (5 s).** "Students moving out list dorm essentials. Students moving in browse by their own hall." Tap **ISR** to show the hall filter, then **All halls**.
2. **Trust (10 s).** Tap the featured **Compact Mini Fridge**. Point to Maya's 4.9 rating, then scroll to **Cleaned. Checked. Ready.**: "Every listing requires a category-specific cleaning checklist." Point to the **Pickup plan**.
3. **Claim (10 s).** Tap **Claim for move-in day** → tap **Fri 20** → **Confirm**. "It's yours!"
4. **My claims (5 s).** Tap **View my claims**. "Reserved for Friday, August 20, at the ISR meeting area. After pickup, they confirm and rate the handoff."

**Optional (20 s): List.** Tap **List an item** → **Try sample** → tap **Publish listing**. The app blocks it. "You can't publish until the cleaning checklist is done." Tick the four steps → **Publish listing** → **You're live.**

**Between visitors:** tap the **AR** avatar → **Reset demo**. (Refreshing the page also works.)

## B. One sentence

Relay is a campus marketplace where UIUC students moving out of the dorms pass along cleaned, checked essentials (mini fridges, lamps, fans, storage, microwaves) to incoming students, who claim them ahead of time and pick them up on move-in day.

## C. Three differentiators

- **Dorm-specific discovery.** Browse by residence area and hall (ISR, FAR, PAR, Ikenberry) instead of scrolling a city-wide marketplace.
- **Standardized cleaning and trust.** Every listing needs a category-specific cleaning checklist, plus seller ratings and an optional Relay-checked second look (proposed).
- **Move-in-oriented claiming.** Claim in spring or summer, pick up during the August move-in window, and track each item from Reserved to Picked up to Complete.

## D. Ten Types of Innovation tactics (proposed)

| Type | Proposed tactic | Where it shows in the prototype |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Network** | Explore partnerships with University Housing, hall councils and campus sustainability groups to reach students at move-out and move-in and to agree on safe handoff points. | Hall filters, campus pickup points, UIUC framing |
| **Process** | A standardized list → clean → claim → pick up flow built around the academic calendar. | Required checklists, soft-goods rules, future-dated claims, status tracking |
| **Service** | A more predictable, trustworthy handoff than a scattered group chat. | Seller ratings, Relay-checked badge, clear condition, pickup confirmation and rating |

These are planned strategies, not existing partnerships.

## E. Honest limitations

- Listings, sellers and ratings are simulated.
- No real transactions, payments or reservations happen.
- Ratings are illustrative.
- Pickup coordination and the "designated meeting areas" are conceptual.
- Housing and campus partnerships are hypothetical; we have no agreements.
- Next steps: talk to real outgoing and incoming students, validate demand, and plan handoff logistics before building further.

## F. Likely judge questions

**1. Why not just use Facebook Marketplace?**
Marketplace is built for local, same-day sales. Relay is built around a student timeline: list at move-out, claim months ahead, pick up at move-in, filtered by your hall. It also sets a cleaning standard that general marketplaces don't.

**2. How would pickup and storage work across semesters?**
That's the hardest open question. Options to test, in order of effort: (1) direct handoff when a seller is still on campus, (2) a friend or roommate holding the item over the summer, (3) a partnership for short-term storage or designated collection points. We'd pilot the simplest option first and learn where it breaks.

**3. How would you prevent dirty or unsafe items?**
We only accept hard-surface essentials. Bedding and soft goods are blocked. Every listing needs a category-specific cleaning checklist, buyers rate each handoff, and an optional Relay-checked second look at pickup is a proposed add-on.

**4. How would you attract enough buyers and sellers?**
Both sides show up at predictable moments: move-out in May and move-in in August. We'd concentrate outreach there, start with one residence area, and seed supply by asking outgoing students before they would otherwise throw items away.

**5. How could Relay sustain itself?**
Ideas to test, none validated: a small service fee on paid items, a paid convenience tier (such as storage or delivery), or sponsorship from campus sustainability programs. Free items would stay free.

**6. What would you test in a first residence-hall pilot?**
In one area (for example ISR): Will outgoing students list items? Will incoming students claim months in advance? What share of claims actually get picked up? Does the cleaning checklist change buyers' trust? Which pickup option works best?
